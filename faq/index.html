Add-ons

They don’t talk to each other directly: your agent is the one that passes the work between them. Each add-on does its one job and hands the result back to your agent, which takes it from there, either passing it along to the next add-on to build on, or calling on several at once and combining what they send back. Every add-on stays focused on its own job; your agent is the one carrying the work from step to step and pulling the pieces into a single answer. Think of it like a kitchen line: each station handles one part of the dish, and your agent is the chef moving the plate from one station to the next and deciding what comes together.
Because “everything” isn’t one job: it’s hundreds of unrelated ones bundled under a single word. That’s exactly why you want many separate add-ons instead. Each handles one job well, so your agent can act sharply, reaching for the right one at the right moment instead of wading past the tax rules and the flight-booking steps just to find your dinner recipe. You still get everything: your agent simply picks the focused add-on that fits the moment. It’s a well-organized toolbox, not one giant gadget welded together.
Each add-on carries a short description of what it’s for. When you hand your agent a task, it scans those descriptions, picks the one that fits, and only then loads the full instructions. That’s why focused add-ons matter: clear, narrow descriptions make the right pick obvious.
